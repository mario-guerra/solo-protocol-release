---

Before you answer, go back and re-read the plan — the actual current version, not your memory of it. Re-read the original request too. Confirm the plan still solves what was actually asked for, not a neighboring problem that's easier to answer.

Now: are you completely happy with this plan, such that you would stand behind it as your absolute best work? If not, what's holding it back? You will be responsible for implementing this, so I need you to be completely confident in your ability to achieve a successful outcome on your first attempt.

Do not check boxes. Do not follow a rubric. Look at the plan as the person who has to build it and tell me what you actually see — what survives scrutiny, what doesn't, and what nobody has brought up yet. If something is wrong, say **HOLD** and state exactly what needs to change before you proceed.

Be honest with yourself about where your confidence is coming from. If you're confident because you actually verified something, say so. If you're confident because the plan "feels right" or because it survived critique and revise, that's not evidence — that's borrowed confidence from a process that can have blind spots. Don't rubber-stamp this with "I'm confident" if you can't point to what you checked. Don't hedge with vague "there may be edge cases" — name the edge case or don't bring it up. And watch for scope drift: plans have a way of quietly solving a slightly different problem than what was originally requested.
